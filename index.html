DexAutoSERL: Automatic Intervention Reinforcement Learning for Real-World Dexterous Manipulation from One Demonstration

CoRL 2026
Overview of DexAutoSERL. (1) Demonstration Phase Segmentation: The demonstration trajectory is divided into different phases. (2) Trajectory Synthesis: An intervention reference trajectory is generated based on the initial object position at episode reset. (3) Dexterous Manipulation Auto Intervention: Consisting of eigengrasp action space, sliding window intervention, and task-agnostic dense reward.

Abstract

Real-world reinforcement learning has emerged as a promising paradigm for robotic manipulation, enabling direct policy learning through physical trial-and-error without requiring explicit modeling of complex contact-rich dynamics. AutoSERL is a pioneer in this direction, demonstrating that a single human demonstration is sufficient to fully automate the intervention process in real-world RL, eliminating the need for continuous human supervision. However, AutoSERL is designed for general end-effector manipulation and does not address the unique challenges of dexterous hands: their high-dimensional action space drastically increases exploration complexity and optimization instability, sparse rewards lead to extremely inefficient learning, and the lack of finger-level guidance results in unstable grasps and frequent task failures. In this work, we present DexAutoSERL, an extension of AutoSERL tailored for real-world dexterous manipulation. We adopt eigengrasps to reduce the 6-dimensional finger action space to a 1-dimensional latent space, significantly lowering exploration difficulty while preserving dominant grasping patterns. We extend the sliding window intervention mechanism to the finger level, providing fine-grained guidance for finger movements in addition to end-effector control. Furthermore, we design a task-agnostic dense reward consisting of finger stabilization, intervention reduction, and training acceleration terms to alleviate the sparse reward problem. We evaluate DexAutoSERL on three challenging dexterous manipulation tasks: plush toy pick-up, orange pick-and-place, and kettle pick-and-pour. Experimental results show that DexAutoSERL consistently outperforms human-intervention-based RL methods (HIL-SERL, ConRFT) and the single-demonstration imitation learning baseline DemoGen. Across all evaluated tasks, DexAutoSERL achieves a 100\% success rate when object positions are randomized within a 6 cm × 6 cm region.
